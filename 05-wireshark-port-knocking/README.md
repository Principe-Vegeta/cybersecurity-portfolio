# Wireshark Port Knocking Lab

A network-analysis write-up based on a controlled training laboratory exercise involving port knocking.

## Objective

Analyze a packet capture to identify a sequence of TCP SYN attempts and understand how the sequence can be used as a trigger for a hidden service in a lab environment.

## Analysis performed

The training exercise used Wireshark to inspect repeated SYN packets sent to the same host. The sequence observed in the lab was:

```text
13 -> 37 -> 30000 -> 3000 -> 1337
```

The exercise demonstrated the relationship between packet-level observation and the behavior of a port-knocking mechanism.

## Skills demonstrated

- Wireshark packet inspection
- TCP SYN analysis
- Network traffic pattern recognition
- Understanding of port-knocking concepts
- Translating packet observations into a testable hypothesis

## Important note

The sequence above belongs to the training exercise documented in my study notes. This repository does not include the original capture, credentials, flags, or third-party target information.

## What I learned

The important skill was not memorizing a port sequence. It was learning to inspect network traffic, identify repeated connection attempts, recognize a meaningful pattern, and document the reasoning that connects the packet evidence to the observed lab behavior.

## Reproduction

To reproduce this type of analysis safely, use a packet capture generated by your own isolated lab or a training platform that explicitly authorizes the exercise. Open the `.pcap` in Wireshark and filter for TCP SYN traffic, then document the relevant source, destination, ports, and timing.
